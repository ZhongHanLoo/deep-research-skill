# Brief
**Question (refined):** For healthy adults, what do large cohort studies, meta-analyses and regulator guidance (2015-2026) say about the benefits and risks of moderate coffee consumption (roughly 2-4 cups/day), and where is the evidence contested?
**Clarifications:** none needed (smoke test).
**Deliverable:** brief answer, 150-300 words before sources, general audience.

## Angles (round 1)
### 1. benefits — mortality and cardiometabolic outcomes
Hypothesis: moderate intake is associated with lower all-cause mortality and lower type 2 diabetes risk in observational cohorts, with no causal proof.
Would disconfirm: large cohorts or Mendelian randomisation showing null or harmful associations at 2-4 cups/day.
First queries: `coffee consumption all-cause mortality meta-analysis`, `coffee type 2 diabetes cohort umbrella review`, `Mendelian randomization coffee`
Source types to prefer: peer-reviewed meta-analyses, umbrella reviews, BMJ/Circulation/NEJM cohorts.

### 2. risks — caffeine limits, subgroups and adverse effects
Hypothesis: regulators put a safe ceiling near 400 mg caffeine/day for adults and 200 mg in pregnancy; risks concentrate in pregnancy, sleep, anxiety and unfiltered coffee (LDL).
Would disconfirm: regulator guidance or trials showing harm at moderate intake in the general adult population.
First queries: `EFSA caffeine safety 400 mg`, `coffee pregnancy guidance 200 mg`, `unfiltered coffee cholesterol cafestol`
Source types to prefer: EFSA, FDA, ACOG, NHS; systematic reviews.

## Plan
Recommended agents: 2 researchers (+1 writer). Execution: parallel.
Sources per angle per round: 3. Max rounds: 1. Verification scope: central claims, max 8.
Stop rule: single round (quick preset).

- Dose-response shape: where do benefits plateau or reverse?
