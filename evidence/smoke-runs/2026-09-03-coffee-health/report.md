# Coffee Consumption and Health in Adults

**Question:** For healthy adults, what does the evidence say on moderate (2-4 cups/day) coffee?   **Depth:** quick preset, 1 round, 22 sources, 8 claims verified.

## Summary
Cohorts link moderate coffee to lower all-cause mortality, lowest near 1 cup/day (J-shaped), fading by 5+ cups [5][11][19]. Diabetes risk falls with dose, similarly for caffeinated and decaf [4][14]. Mendelian randomization finds no causal cardiovascular benefit, suggesting confounding in the cohorts [7][17]. FDA/EFSA treat up to 400 mg/day caffeine as safe [6][13][21]. Unfiltered coffee raises LDL cholesterol [10][22]; caffeine dose-dependently raises anxiety [9][18]. Pregnancy limits are disputed [8][20].

## Key findings
| # | Finding | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~12% lower mortality, 4-5 cups/day, smoking-adjusted | high | [5][15] |
| 2 | J-shaped mortality curve, lowest at ~1 cup/day | high | [11][19] |
| 3 | Diabetes risk falls with dose, RR 0.92→0.67 | high | [4][14] |
| 4 | MR: no causal link, coffee to 15 CV outcomes | high | [7][17] |
| 5 | Caffeine dose-dependently raises anxiety | high | [9][18] |
| 6 | Unfiltered coffee raises LDL; filtered mostly spares it | high | [10][22] |

## Disagreements and contradictions
Cohorts tie coffee to lower cardiovascular death [5][11], but Mendelian randomization found no causal link across 15 outcomes [7], corroborated independently [17] — disputed, per the MR authors, by confounding [7]. Pregnancy guidance is contradicted: ACOG says moderate caffeine (<200 mg/day) is not a major miscarriage/preterm-birth factor [8], but a 2020 review of 48 studies finds no clearly safe level, though ACOG's guidance stood unchanged [20].

## What this report could not find
Whether decaf matches caffeinated coffee's mortality effect (only diabetes was compared) [4]; the exact dose benefit reverses past 5 cups/day.
