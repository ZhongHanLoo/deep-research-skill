# Angle

**Angle:** risks — caffeine limits, subgroups and adverse effects

## Hypothesis / Would disconfirm
**Hypothesis:** Regulators put a safe ceiling near 400 mg caffeine/day for adults and 200 mg in pregnancy; risks concentrate in pregnancy, sleep, anxiety and unfiltered coffee (LDL).
**Would disconfirm:** Regulator guidance or trials showing harm at moderate intake in the general adult population.

## Claims extracted
- c001 (central, [1]): EFSA — single caffeine doses up to 200 mg raise no safety concern for adults.
- c002 (central, [1]): EFSA — habitual caffeine intake up to 400 mg/day raises no safety concern for non-pregnant adults.
- c003 (central, [1]): EFSA — habitual caffeine intake up to 200 mg/day by pregnant women raises no safety concern for the fetus.
- c004 (supporting, [1]): EFSA — single/habitual caffeine up to 200 mg by lactating women raises no safety concern for breastfed infants.
- c005 (supporting, [1]): EFSA — evidence insufficient to derive a safe caffeine intake for children and adolescents.
- c006 (supporting, [1]): EFSA — alcohol up to ~0.65 g/kg bw does not affect the safety of single 200 mg caffeine doses.
- c007 (central, [5]): ACOG — moderate caffeine intake under 200 mg/day does not cause miscarriage or preterm birth.
- c008 (supporting, [5]): ACOG — a 12-oz cup of coffee contains about 200 mg caffeine, the pregnancy limit.
- c009 (supporting, [5]): ACOG — caffeine can interfere with sleep and contribute to nausea and light-headedness in pregnancy.
- c010 (supporting, [5]): ACOG — caffeine can increase urination and lead to dehydration in pregnancy.
- c011 (central, [6]): Meta-analysis of 14 RCTs found a dose-response relation between coffee intake and total/LDL cholesterol (p<0.01).
- c012 (central, [6]): Unfiltered but not filtered coffee increases serum total and LDL cholesterol.
- c013 (supporting, [6]): Filtered coffee trials showed very little increase in serum cholesterol.
- c014 (supporting, [6]): Lipid increases from coffee were larger in hyperlipidemic patients and with caffeinated or boiled coffee.

## Verdict on hypothesis
**Mixed / largely supported.** The EFSA ceiling figures (400 mg/day general adults, 200 mg/day pregnancy, 200 mg single dose) and ACOG's independent 200 mg/day pregnancy figure confirm the regulatory-ceiling half of the hypothesis exactly, and the RCT meta-analysis confirms the unfiltered-coffee/LDL mechanism. No source found showed regulator guidance or trial evidence of harm from moderate intake in the general (non-pregnant, non-hyperlipidemic) adult population, so nothing disconfirms the hypothesis; the "sleep" and "anxiety" parts of the hypothesis are only thinly evidenced here (one non-quantified ACOG sentence on sleep, and no dedicated anxiety source), leaving that part of the hypothesis untested rather than confirmed.

## Gaps / suggested sub-questions
- No dedicated source on caffeine and anxiety in the general (non-pregnant) adult population was fetched; a candidate for round 2 is PMC8951500 ("Caffeine Intake and Its Sex-Specific Association with General Anxiety") or an FDA statement on caffeine and anxiety/insomnia.
- The EFSA abstract page gave the headline figures but not the full opinion's per-subgroup dose-response detail (e.g. exact thresholds cited for cardiovascular or CNS endpoints); the full EFSA Journal PDF/HTML (behind Wiley) was not fetched.
- No source quantifies the magnitude of LDL increase from unfiltered coffee in mg/dL or % (the meta-analysis abstract gives direction and significance, not effect size); a companion table in the full Jee et al. paper or a more recent meta-analysis (e.g. Cai et al. 2012, PMID 22713771) could fill this.
- FDA's own caffeine guidance (as distinct from EFSA, an EU body) was not fetched; a US-specific regulator source would strengthen the "regulators" plural claim for a US-audience report.
