## v1.4 candidates (from the v1.3 confirmation run, read offline 2026-09-11; progress #66)

W1. **Writer: draft-to-budget instead of draft-then-cut.** On the v1.3 confirmation run the outline's budgets summed to 1,520 words and the first draft came out at 2,359 (+55%), trimmed to 1,592 over twelve rounds; on the v1.2 run of the same brief the outline summed to 1,470 and the final report was 1,734 (+18%) with the timeline table at 307 words against a 150 budget. The mechanism is visible in the outlines: sections are assigned far more claims than their budget can carry (v1.3: 17 claim ids to a 150-word section, 15 to a 250-word one, about 74 claim mentions over 950 words of body; v1.2: about 63 over 1,070), and because every central claim must be used, the draft grows to cite them all and is then compressed. Two fixes to test: (a) a density rule at the outline stage, at most one claim per 15-20 words of budget, with surplus central claims placed in a table row or a one-line "also established" list rather than prose; (b) a per-section word check (`wc -w` on the section) before starting the next section, so the writer drafts to the budget rather than cutting afterwards. Cost of the writer stage on the confirmation run: 221k against 164k.
 **Designed 2026-09-11 evening (progress #68), not applied; exact text.** In `prompts/writer.md`, "Outline first", after "budgets summing to the length target": *"Assign claim ids to a section in proportion to its budget, at most one id per 20 words (a 150-word section carries seven or eight); a central claim that does not fit its section's budget goes into a key-findings or timeline row, or into a one-line 'also established' list at the end of the section, never into an extra paragraph."* And in "Rules", after the length sentence: *"After drafting each section, count its words (`wc -w` on the section, or the script's count) and trim it to its budget before starting the next section; the whole draft is never longer than the target plus 10%."*

W3. **Writer: use supporting claims that define or classify a named entity.** On the business-finance-3 confirmation (progress #67) the C1 line registered the insurer's status sentence as a supporting claim and the writer left it unused; the item failed. The writer's coverage rule reads central claims and the question-coverage table names only what the question names. Two fixes to test: (a) a writer step, before finalising, that scans the supporting claims for statements of what kind of body each named entity is, the threshold or rule it applies, and the grade attached to a recommendation, and places each in one clause; (b) a row per named entity in the outline's question-coverage table ("what it is / status"), which makes the gap visible when no claim carries it. Cost: a few sentences of prompt; no contract change.
 **Designed 2026-09-11 evening (progress #68), not applied; exact text.** (a) In "Outline first", after the question-coverage table sentence: *"Add one row per body, programme, rule or threshold the question names, headed 'what it is / status', with the claim id that says what kind of body it is, the level it treats as protective, the rule that applies instead, or the grade attached to it; these are usually `supporting` claims. A row with no claim is a gap like any other."* (b) In "Rules", in the self-check bullet after the `supporting-claim-unused` pass: *"and one pass over the 'what it is / status' rows of your coverage table: each fact there is one clause in the section that first names the entity, cited to its claim."* **Offline test before any confirmation run (about 0.5M):** two writer agents on scratch copies of the `2026-09-11-B2/business-finance-3` run folder, control with the current prompt and treatment with W1 and W3, same ledger; measure whether the treatment cites the insurer's status claim (c082), the first-draft and final word counts, and the judge score of each report under the current judge line (two Opus judges, about 120k); the earlier run's writer left c082 unused and drafted 1,928 words.
