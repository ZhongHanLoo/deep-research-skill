## What the seven have in common, and what they do not

1. **Four of seven sit inside the span the researcher was reading** (five counting the science-health-3 sentence, which was registered; see point 6), between or next to registered quotes, on a page that was already at the per-source central cap and carried 8-14 registered claims, at or above the top of the prompt's "2-8 claims per source" range. The researcher read the sentence (in three cases it is in the same paragraph as a registered quote, in one the same line) and did not register it.
2. **Every one of those five is a definitional, status, threshold or general-rule sentence**, not a question-specific number or date: what body the insurer is, what coverage level protects a community, what the statute says about the standard for policymaking and factfinding, what a shared cache must not do with an authenticated request, what the guideline recommends instead and at what grade. The researcher prompt says "prefer numbers, dates, named entities, mechanisms and explicit findings over generalities", and each of these reads as a generality to a researcher whose hypothesis is about the numbers. The v1.1 adjacent-rule instruction ("extract every normative rule, recommendation, holding, exception and carve-out in the sections you cite ... and the companion statements") covers them in words and did not produce them in four runs after it was added.
3. **One (policy-law-3 r4) is a far section of a long document**, which the v1.1 section-list rule targets; the opinion is a PDF with no section list to scan, and the doctrine sits in a part of the opinion the angle's hypothesis did not name.
4. **One (technology-1 r7) is on verifier-added pages only.** Verifiers fetch pages to corroborate existing claims and register no claims of their own; four of the five class-1 facts also sat on such pages (an MDN reference, a family-medicine digest, the insurer's FAQ, four health-news pages), so a verifier that could register a supporting claim would have had a second chance at five of seven.
5. **The gap-hunter experiment (progress #56) is consistent with this**: it caught the one miss that a generic "nature of each compared entity" clause reaches (the insurer's status) and none of the others, because its checklist came from the question and brief; a checklist is not the lever, the extraction habit on the page is.
6. **Correction to the record:** the science-health-3 miss was not "a passage in no claim". The recommendation was registered as a supporting claim in round 1 and added to the report by the coverage pass; the item failed on its other two parts (grade letter, population), which the claim's quote does not contain. Progress #56 and the backlog described it as absent from the ledger; this note supersedes that description. Checked further on 2026-09-11 afternoon: the grade letter attached to that recommendation is on none of the run's fetched pages (it sits on the body's separate falls-prevention statement, which no angle fetched), so that part of the item is a page-not-fetched miss, not an adjacent-passage miss.

## Candidates this supports (not applied; skill and prompts unchanged)

- **C1. Researcher prompt, extraction step (class 1, five of seven):** add a per-source instruction that the page's own sentences that define or classify an entity, threshold or rule named in the question (what the body is, the level that counts as protection, the rule that applies unless or instead, the grade attached to a recommendation) are to be registered as `supporting` claims even when they read as general, and qualify "over generalities" to exclude them. Cost: `supporting` claims are quote-checked only, so a handful per source adds a few thousand tokens per run and no corroboration. Risk: more claims per source (the 2026-09-10 cap re-test showed prompts that name a number anchor researchers; this one names a kind, not a number).
- **C2. Verifier prompt (class 3, and a second chance at class 1):** when a page fetched for corroboration states a fact about an entity named in the question that the claims list lacks (a date, a status, a threshold), register it as a `supporting` claim on that source. Contract question: v1.1 lets verifiers add and grade sources; claim registration by verifiers is a small extension of the same rule.
- **C3. Section-list rule for documents without one (class 2):** for a judgment or long opinion, list its parts from the table of contents in the syllabus or from the "Part I / II / III" markers and take every part whose subject the question names. Weakest evidence: one miss.
- **Offline test for C1 before any prompt change:** the five class-1 pages are cached. Give a Sonnet agent each page's raw text, the question and the current extraction instruction (control), and the same with C1's line (treatment), and count whether the missed sentence is registered; ten agents at about 40k each, about 0.4M tokens. A positive result on cached pages does not show the line survives a full run's cap and stop-rule pressure; a confirmation run on a stable question (about 1.9M) would still be needed before tagging.

Sources: run folders under `eval/private/results/` (private); progress.md #45, #47, #49, #51, #56, #57, #58, #60.

## Offline test of C1 (2026-09-11, 13:49-13:54 BST; progress #61)

Ten Sonnet agents, one page each, two arms differing only in the C1 line (template `eval/experiments/extraction-c1.md`; filled prompts and outputs private). Each agent had the question, the fetching angle's hypothesis and the page's raw text, and wrote a table of claims with verbatim quotes; no search, no fetch, no ledger, no cap, no source target. Cost 805k tokens (61-112k per agent), twice the 0.4M estimate: the agents read whole pages of 22-242 KB.

Quotes verbatim on the page (first 70 characters): 210 of 220 rows; the misses are ellipsed or curly-quote spans.

**Reading.** Treatment registered the target on four of five pages, control on two (plus a cross-reference on a third); the two pages the line turned were the two shortest, where the control arm had extracted the fewest claims. Two cautions outweigh the tally. First, one sample per arm per page: the opinion and brochure controls caught sentences the live runs missed, so the offline setting (one page, no cap, no stop rule, 14-32 claims against the live runs' 8-14) is more permissive than a run, and a single-page test cannot show that the line survives cap and stop-rule pressure. Second, the RFC rule was registered by neither arm, so the class has at least one member that a wording change does not reach on its own. What the test does support: the C1 line is cheap (every added target claim came out as `supporting`, quote-check only), it does not inflate the central count (the brochure treatment marked 4 central against the control's 16; the RFC and guideline treatments marked about as many as their controls), and it makes the model register definitional sentences it otherwise skips on short pages. Decision left to the user: apply C1 as the first v1.3 change and confirm on one stable question (about 1.5-1.9M), or leave the class open.
